The **International Sustainability Standards Board** has established **IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures** to guide organizations in reporting environmental risks and opportunities that impact financial outlooks over various timeframes. Effective for annual reporting periods beginning in 2024 when paired with **IFRS S1**, these guidelines require companies to share transparent data regarding governance, operational strategies, risk assessment procedures, and climate targets. Built upon the foundational four-pillar framework of the **Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures**, the standard ensures that investors receive globally comparable, decision-useful information. Furthermore, the **ISSB** has introduced targeted amendments to refine **greenhouse gas emissions reporting** and minimize complex, duplicative compliance burdens for entities during implementation. Organizations already utilizing existing **TCFD** or **CFD** mandates possess a robust starting point for these disclosures, though the new regulations demand greater granular detail.

What four content areas does IFRS S2 require entities to disclose?

Under IFRS S2, entities are required to disclose information across **four core content areas** (structured around the four-pillar framework adapted from TCFD)[1][2]:

1. **Governance**: The governance processes, controls, and procedures the entity uses to monitor, manage, and oversee climate-related risks and opportunities[1][3].
2. **Strategy**: The entity's strategy for managing climate-related risks and opportunities over the short, medium, and long term[1].
3. **Risk Management**: The processes the entity uses to identify, assess, prioritize, and monitor climate-related risks and opportunities, including how those processes are integrated into and inform the entity's overall risk management system[1][5].
4. **Metrics and Targets**: The entity's performance in relation to its climate-related risks and opportunities, including progress toward set climate targets (such as Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions) and targets required by law or regulation[1].

### Scope and Applicability (Who Must Report)

* **IFRS S2 Standard**: Applies broadly to **any reporting entity exposed to climate-related physical or transition risks, or that has climate-related opportunities available to it**[1]. As an international framework created by the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB), IFRS S2 sets a global baseline[4]; individual national jurisdictions and regulators determine which domestic entities are legally bound to apply it[7][8].
* **Australian Reporting Regime**: Applies specifically to **entities required to prepare and lodge financial reports under Chapter 2M of the Australian** **Corporations Act 2001** that meet defined size thresholds or have emissions reporting obligations under the National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Scheme (NGERS)[9]. Entities not meeting these thresholds or exempt from Chapter 2M lodging are explicitly excluded[10].

---

### Implementation Timeline (When to Report)

* **IFRS S2 Standard**: Effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after **1 January 2024**, with earlier application permitted provided IFRS S1 is also applied[1][11].
* **Australian Reporting Regime**: Introduces a **phased rollout starting 1 January 2025** under the *Treasury Laws Amendment (Financial Market Infrastructure and Other Measures) Act 2024*[10]:
  * **Group 1**: Financial years commencing on or after **1 January 2025**[10].
  * **Group 2**: Financial years commencing on or after **1 July 2026**[10].
  * **Group 3**: Financial years commencing on or after **1 July 2027**[10].
